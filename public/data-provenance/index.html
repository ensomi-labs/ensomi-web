R2 · data provenance

Where R2's data comes from

Which charts and music ensomi R2 was built from, and what for; what our audit shows and does not show; what we decided; and how to ask for a correction.

  • Last updated 2026-10-21
  • Audit evidence: snapshot of 2026-10-17
  • Policy statement: 2026-10-21

This page covers ensomi R2 1.0 and 1.0.1, the releases r2-1.0 and r2-1.0.1 on the model card, and no earlier model such as R1. It summarises our data provenance report, the detailed reference, with its history of changes.

What R2 used, and what for

R2 was built from osu!'s ranked and loved osu!mania 4K charts from 2 to 6 stars. We do not distribute these charts or their audio with the model. How the parts below fit together is in the R2 report.

  • Training. 11,368 eligible training charts train two models. The rows model learns where notes start, from music features together with the mappers' own timing and note-start positions. R2 learns how to arrange notes from the charts' rows; it does not hear the audio. Both were trained from scratch, without weights from an earlier model.
  • Corpus prior. The corpus prior takes a new chart's character, such as how many long notes it has, from training charts whose notes fall in a similar way. The published prior.json keeps one row of numbers for each training chart, without names; it still derives from those charts.
  • Calibration. The playability calibration and the scope table, published with the model, hold numbers derived from the training charts.
  • Timing. The mappers' tempo, beat grid and note positions served as references for choosing how R2 fits a beat grid to a song's audio. This set the method's choices; it trained no additional neural model.
  • Evaluation and listening. Some charts were selected as references for the panels that measure generated charts, and for listening comparisons.
  • Previews. The four previews published with the model use the audio of four source songs. The report names the source charts' mappers; they are not the authors of the generated arrangements.
  • Beat tracking. R2 uses BeatThis final0, a beat tracker pretrained by its own authors, as is, without further training. Its training data comes from its authors' own sources, and the record of the musicians in that data is incomplete.

The numbers, and what they are not

These count the 11,368 eligible training charts as the audit found them on 2026-10-17. Labels are claims in the charts' metadata, not verified people or rights holders.

11,368 charts
A chart is one difficulty: one distinct .osu file.
1,129 Creator labels
Distinct mapper names as the charts give them. Aliases are not merged, collaborations are not split, and a guest difficulty's mapper may be missing.
2,445 Artist labels
Distinct artist names as the charts give them. Not a list of composers, performers or rights holders; “Various Artists” is one label.
5,026 audio hashes
Distinct audio files, by a recorded hash of each file's exact bytes. Not songs: different files of the same song count separately.
4,167 groups
Groups used to keep related sources within the same data partition. Not songs either.

Eligible means a chart was in the pool that training drew from. The records do not show whether or how often a chart was drawn, or what it contributed to the models. The full frozen inventory the audit started from holds 22,066 charts; it is not the training population.

What the audit shows, and what it does not

The audit establishes which charts, and which Creator and Artist labels, each recorded stage of R2 used, from training and calibration to evaluation and previews. Each stage's records are tied to their charts by the exact hash of the chart file, and every record matched.

It leaves these open:

  • rights clearance: it does not establish permission to use the charts or the music;
  • a complete review of how the charts and music were acquired, and of permissions;
  • who the mappers and musicians behind the labels are;
  • which charts training actually drew, and how often;
  • a complete list of the musicians in BeatThis's training data.

What we decided

Policy statement of 2026-10-21

The released models r2-1.0 and r2-1.0.1 remain available, with this disclosure of their data provenance.

Future released models will use data covered by permissive licences or by explicit permission suitable for the intended training and release uses. The data review for such a release considers, as applicable, inherited weights, upstream components such as pretrained beat trackers, distillation from earlier models, and derived data such as priors and calibration tables, not only the training charts.

This policy is separate from the audit, which does not show that the data behind R2 meets it.

Enrollment and opt-out

We are exploring contributor enrollment (opt-in) and opt-out requests, including a convenient way to verify that a decision comes from the relevant chart or music rights holder. No such process is in place, and its effect on existing models has not been established. We do not claim to remove what a released model has already learned from any source.

Check the tables

stage_counts.csv gives each stage's counts of charts, groups, audio hashes and labels, and the tables' README explains every column. Two label tables, creator_labels_by_stage.csv and artist_labels_by_stage.csv, will list every exact Creator and Artist label in each stage. They are not published yet.

Once they are, to look for a name, filter stage, for example to r2_cache_fit_train for the 11,368 training charts, then search raw_label, also for part of the name, since aliases are not merged and collaborations are not split. Other stages, such as the development pool, the evaluation panels and the previews, have their own ids. Stages overlap, so never add their counts together.

Corrections and questions

To correct an attribution, to ask how a chart or song was used, or to say you are interested in enrollment or opt-out, open a data provenance issue on GitHub. English, Japanese and Chinese are all welcome.

Open a data provenance issue

Issues are public: include no personal data and no audio. A beatmap link or ID, or a label as the tables show it, is enough. Opening an issue does not by itself remove anything from a released model.
