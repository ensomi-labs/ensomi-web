Why we are building ensomi

Most of the time, music is something we listen to. A rhythm game lets you step inside a song: your hands follow the beat, and listening turns into playing. But a song becomes playable only after someone charts it by hand, note by note, and most of the music in your life never gets a chart.

We want to change that. ensomi learns from charts that mappers have made and writes new ones from the audio, so that the music you love, and the music already around you, can become something you play.

What we are building

We want listening to music, playing it and making charts for it to become one activity. Four moments show what we mean.

Ambient play

The music around you becomes something you play.

A song is already playing nearby. ensomi recognises it, finds where it is, and starts a chart, generated in real time, that follows the music without interrupting it.

Mapping

You shape the choreography; ensomi offers candidates.

A mapper selects one passage and asks for candidate choreography while the rest of the chart stays in place. They compare, keep, retry or undo. The choices, and the expression, stay with the mapper.

Training

Practise on music you love.

A player picks songs they love and a movement they want to get better at, and the chart is shaped around that movement.

Playing together

Your best play lands in your opponent's chart.

Like clearing lines in versus Tetris, a clean passage or a long combo sends an attack: a burst of notes or a harder pattern, dropped into what your opponent is about to play. In co-op you coordinate instead, and what one of you plays shapes the other's chart.

We are not there yet. Each moment needs charts that are playable at the difficulty you ask for, follow the music, keep one character through a whole song and answer what you ask. R2 is our first step: a whole song, charted while you wait, and you can already ask for a style in one part of it. It does not yet play along in real time, change a chart while someone plays it, or rework one passage of an existing chart.

R2: from a song to a chart you can play

R2 is our first publicly playable release. It works from the audio alone, and between your song and your chart, three things happen.

  1. It finds the beat.R2 listens for the beats, then lines each tempo section up with where the song's sounds begin.
  2. It chooses where notes fall.Beat by beat, it decides where notes start, from what it hears.
  3. It arranges the chart.It takes a character, such as how many long notes and chords, from human charts whose notes fall in a similar way, then lays out lanes, taps and long notes. This step does not hear the audio yet.

What comes out is a complete osu!mania 4K chart, made in your browser, that you can watch against the music and open in osu!.

R2 and the rows model were trained on osu!'s ranked and loved 4K charts from 2 to 6 stars. We do not distribute those charts or their audio.

Next: R3

We are now building R3, the next system. Like R2, it spans timing, head rows (where notes fall) and choreography (how they are arranged). It goes after what R2 taught us, in this order.

  1. Notes on the music

    Notes should land on what you hear. We are working on head rows that sit more precisely on what the audio actually contains.

  2. An arrangement that listens

    R2's arrangement does not hear the audio. It lays out notes from where they fall, not from the sound, and it shows in long notes that do not follow the music. For R3 we are bringing music information into the arrangement model, so that the choreography listens too.

  3. Less hidden steering

    A few calibration rules hold R2 steady as it writes, such as a restoring force that pulls the chart back toward the character it was given. We are reviewing them, to find where they bend particular charts out of shape.

  4. Difficulty you can trust

    When you ask for a star rating, the chart should match it. Today a chart can come out easier or harder than you asked. We are working on better difficulty control, and getting it right may reshape much of the system.

These are aims, and the work is in progress. As with R2, we will say what R3 does only once we have checked it.

Run it yourself

The generator above runs R2 in your browser with @ensomi/runtime: the networks in ONNX Runtime Web, everything else in ensomi's Rust runtime compiled to WebAssembly. The browser decodes the song and draws its own random numbers, so a seed here does not give the same chart as the same seed on the command line.

To run the release from the command line (we checked it on macOS with Apple silicon; Linux and CUDA were not run):

git clone https://github.com/ensomi-labs/ensomi-model.git
cd ensomi-model
git checkout --detach r2-1.0.1
uv sync --python 3.10 --extra mps
hf download sed-i/ensomi-r2 --revision r2-1.0.1 --local-dir artifacts/hf-r2

uv run --python 3.10 --extra mps python -m ensomi_model.system.make \
  --model-dir artifacts/hf-r2 --audio /path/to/song.mp3 --star 3.5 \
  --title "Song title" --artist "Artist" --seed 0 --out artifacts/my-chart.osz

The first run also downloads BeatThis's checkpoint (81 MB) from its authors. Decoding audio other than WAV needs ffmpeg. --help lists every request. Full instructions are on the Hugging Face card.
